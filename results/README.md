# Results

- `phase0/`: reproduction of the first-pass numbers (inventory, overlap rule, error rates, the first metric cut); `NUMBERS.md` at the repo root maps each number to its script.
- `phase1/`: the prior-work search records. The files and their columns are described below.
- `phase2/`: the analysis. `comparisons.csv` is the pre-registered comparison table; `rq1_rules.json` the rule error rates; `rq2_summary.json` and `rq2_comparisons.csv` the evidence per comparison; `rq3_*.json` the runs needed, the paired per-task analysis and the injected-defect study; `figures/` the three figures; `NUMBERS_phase2.md` maps every number quoted in the memos to its output.

The phase 0 and phase 2 outputs regenerate with `scripts/run_phase0.sh` and `scripts/run_phase2.sh`. The phase 1 records are not generated by a script. They were compiled by hand during the search and are dated 2026-10-11.

## Phase 1 records

- `phase1_summary.json`: the search counts and the list of second-round searches. `n_searches` counts the six first-round searches and the eight second-round searches (14 in all). `n_candidate_records` is the number of rows in `phase1_candidates.csv`. `n_verified_at_source` is the number of rows in `phase1_verdicts.csv`.
- `phase1_candidates.csv`: one row per candidate screened (381 rows). The same source can appear in several rows when more than one search found it.
- `phase1_verdicts.csv`: one row per source read in full at source (24 rows), with a verdict.
- `extra_verifications/`: notes on sources checked at source outside the verdict table: arXiv 2608.15046, arXiv 2609.13714 with ClawBench issue #359, arXiv 2610.04433, and the software-engineering references on equivalence testing. The notes on papers use the same verdict labels.

### Columns in `phase1_candidates.csv`

- `overlap_risk`: how much the source could pre-empt this study.
  - `none`: unrelated, or only a name or keyword match.
  - `low`: a neighbour. Related topic, but no overlap with the planned contributions.
  - `medium`: touches the parity rule, the parity data or the proposed protocol. It must be cited or positioned against.
  - `high`: would pre-empt a contribution. No record received this rating.
- `read_depth`: how far the source was read.
  - `title`: title, listing entry or search-result snippet only.
  - `abstract`: the abstract page, sometimes with snippets of the full text.
  - `full`: the full text, or the full thread, page or file.
- `type`: `paper`, `pr` (pull request), `issue`, `docs`, `blog`, `post`, `repo`, `dataset-discussion` or `other` (talks, podcasts and other pages).
- `date`: the publication, posting or event date given by the source. For live documents (repository files, web pages) it is the date of the last change or the date the page was read, as stated in the cell.
- `source`: the site, venue or repository the record comes from.
- `summary` and `why`: what the source does, and why it matters for this study.

Notes such as "verify in full text", "snippet only", "not opened" or "full text not read" mark candidates screened from an abstract or a search-result snippet. They were not read in full. Summaries that begin "Corrected citation" give author lists, dates and venues re-checked at the source.

### Columns in `phase1_verdicts.csv`

- `verdict`: the judgement after reading the source in full.
  - `narrows`: the source already does part of what this study plans. The novelty claim must be narrowed and the source cited.
  - `neighbor`: related work that does not do what this study does. Cite it and state the difference.
  - `irrelevant`: not prior work on this study's question. Some of these rows are primary sources (Harbor pull requests and a parity post on the Hugging Face dataset) that the paper uses as examples, not as related work.
- `confidence`: confidence in the verdict, from 0 to 1.
- `read_depth`: always `full` in this file.
- `analyzes_harbor_parity_data`, `critiques_range_overlap_rule`, `applies_equivalence_testing_to_benchmark_ports`, `compares_reimplemented_benchmark_to_original_statistically`: `True` or `False`, for whether the source does each of these things.
- `what_it_does`, `what_it_leaves_open`: the reading notes.
- `citation`: a BibTeX entry.

### Shorthand in the notes

- "Contribution (1)", "(2)" and "(3)" are the study's three planned contributions: (1) the error rates of Harbor's range-overlap rule (RQ1); (2) a statistical re-analysis of the published parity data (RQ2); (3) a protocol with a stated equivalence margin, the runs needed and a paired per-task analysis (RQ3).
- Some summaries close with a three-part check: (a) does the source analyse Harbor parity, (b) does it study ports or re-implementations of a benchmark, (c) does it use equivalence testing.
- "k" is the number of runs per side.
